Water Problems We See in Taylorsville Homes

Older homes and aging plumbing

Taylorsville has streets of 1950s brick homes plus many houses from the 1970s through the 1990s. Galvanized pipes, original shut-off valves and older water heaters fail with age. See burst pipe water damage and water heater leak cleanup.

Condos, townhomes and apartments

About a quarter of Taylorsville's housing is multi-family. A washer hose, toilet line or water heater failure in one unit can soak the ceilings and walls of the units below. We trace water through every affected unit and document each one separately for owners, tenants and HOAs.

High Jordan River flows

In high-water years, an over-full Jordan River has pushed water back up through storm drains in parts of the west side. Homes near the river can also see groundwater seep into basements during runoff.

Sewer backups

Older clay sewer laterals in mid-century homes crack and fill with tree roots, and backups usually show up first at basement floor drains.

Taylorsville Water Damage FAQs

How fast can you reach Taylorsville?

Taylorsville is a short drive from our Midvale base. We send a crew as soon as you call, day or night.

Water is coming through my ceiling from the unit above. What do I do?

Tell the upstairs neighbor and your HOA or manager to shut off their water, move your belongings, take photos and call us. We dry your unit and document the source for both insurance policies.

Do you work with HOAs and property managers?

Yes. We provide unit-by-unit moisture maps, photos and repair scopes for owners, tenants and adjusters.

Why does my older Taylorsville home keep having leaks?

Aging galvanized pipes corrode from the inside and fail at joints. A plumber can repipe; we handle the drying and repairs.

Is a leak from the unit above covered by insurance?

Often the unit owner's or HOA's policy covers it, depending on the source. We document everything so the right policy can be used.

Do you handle water damage in mobile and manufactured homes?

Yes. Taylorsville has several manufactured home communities. Water under these homes often soaks the subfloor and the insulation in the belly, so we dry from below as well as inside.

Can a leak in a Taylorsville condo spread to the unit next door?

Yes, through shared walls and floors. We check the units beside and below the leak with moisture meters, not just the one where the water started.
