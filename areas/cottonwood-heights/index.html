Water Problems We See in Cottonwood Heights Homes

Ice dams on snowy roofs

Higher elevation means heavier snow and more freeze-thaw cycles on the roof. Ice dams back water up under shingles and into attics, upper walls and ceilings. See ice dam water damage.

Frozen pipes in colder homes

Cold canyon air and older insulation put pipes on exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces at risk every winter. See burst pipe water damage.

Split-level and tri-level lower floors

Homes built to step down sloping lots often have family rooms partly below ground. Snowmelt running downhill can push water into those lower levels through window wells and the base of walls.

Ceiling leaks from upstairs bathrooms

Multi-level homes put bathrooms over living spaces. A failed toilet seal or shower valve can show up as a ceiling stain one floor down.

Cottonwood Heights Water Damage FAQs

How fast can you reach Cottonwood Heights?

Cottonwood Heights borders Midvale, so we reach most of the city quickly, day or night, including during winter storms.

Why do ice dams happen so often in Cottonwood Heights?

Higher elevation brings more snow and more freeze-thaw cycles, and older attics often lose heat that melts roof snow.

Why does water get into my split-level's lower floor?

Lower levels sit partly below the ground. Snowmelt and storm water running downhill can push in through window wells and the base of walls.

Is ice dam damage covered by insurance?

Water damage inside the home from an ice dam is often covered. Ice removal and roof wear may not be.

Do you serve businesses in Cottonwood Heights?

Yes, including offices along Fort Union Boulevard.

Can heavy snow on my roof cause leaks even without an ice dam?

Yes. Deep, wet snow can push water past worn flashing, vent boots and skylights. If a ceiling stain shows up during a big storm week, check the attic and call before the drywall softens.

Do you serve the Butler and Golden Hills neighborhoods?

Yes. We cover all of Cottonwood Heights, including the Butler and Golden Hills areas and the homes up toward Wasatch Boulevard.
