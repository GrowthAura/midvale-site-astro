Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Midvale, Step by Step

Water damage restoration has five steps: inspect, remove the water, dry the structure, clean and protect, then repair. Our Midvale crew follows the same process on every job, guided by the IICRC S500 water damage standard, and you get photos and moisture readings at each step. Call (385) 488-1110.

(385) 488-1110

Quick answer: Most homes go from first call to dry in about 3 to 5 days. Repairs come after that, once readings prove every wet material is dry. The order matters: rebuilding before the structure is dry traps water inside walls and floors.

Step 1: Your Call and Our Inspection

When you call (385) 488-1110, we ask what happened, where the water is and whether it is still running. We help you shut off the water if needed and send a crew. On site we:

  • Check power, gas and structural safety
  • Find and confirm the water source
  • Identify the water category: clean, gray or black
  • Map moisture with meters and a thermal camera
  • Photograph every affected area

Step 2: Water Removal

We extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extractors and submersible pumps. Materials that cannot be saved, like soaked carpet pad or contaminated drywall, are removed and listed for your claim. See emergency water removal.

Step 3: Structural Drying

We set dehumidifiers and air movers sized to your rooms, and use wall cavity drying and floor drying mats when they let us save more of your home. Every day we record moisture readings until each material matches a dry reference. See structural drying.

Step 4: Cleaning and Protection

We clean affected surfaces, apply antimicrobial where needed, control odors and run HEPA air scrubbers for contaminated water or mold. Sewage and flood water get full sanitizing.

Step 5: Repair and Rebuild

Once everything is dry, we replace drywall, match texture and paint, and install flooring, trim and cabinets. One team finishes the job. See water damage repair.

What You Receive From Us

  • Photos of every affected area, before, during and after
  • A moisture map from day one
  • Daily drying logs with readings
  • A list of removed and damaged items

Restoration Process FAQs

How many days does water damage restoration take?

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days. Repairs follow and can take a few days to a few weeks, depending on what was removed.

Do I need to be home during restoration?

You or someone you trust should be there for the first visit and the final walk-through. During drying, we can work with a key or lockbox.

What is the difference between mitigation and restoration?

Mitigation stops the damage from getting worse: water removal, drying and cleaning. Restoration puts the home back: drywall, flooring, paint and repairs. We do both.

Why can't you start repairs right away?

Closing up walls or laying new floors over damp materials traps water inside and leads to mold and warping. We rebuild only after readings confirm dry.

Will you explain each step to me?

Yes. We walk you through the plan on day one and update you every day while equipment is running.

Will you move my furniture?

Yes. We move or block up furniture so floors can dry and nothing stains. Fragile or valuable items are handled with you.

What if you find more damage during drying?

We show you the readings and photos, explain what changed, and update the plan and your claim documentation before doing extra work.

Do you pack out and clean my belongings?

When a room needs major work, we can move furniture and boxes to a dry area or off site, clean what can be saved, and list what cannot for your claim. Items come back once the room is rebuilt.

What equipment will be in my home?

Usually dehumidifiers, air movers, and sometimes floor mats, wall cavity dryers or HEPA air scrubbers. We place them to keep walkways and doors clear, and they run on normal household outlets.

How do I know when my home is truly dry?

When our readings on every wet material match the dry reference reading from an unaffected area. We show you those final numbers before equipment is removed.
