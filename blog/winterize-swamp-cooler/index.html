How to Winterize a Swamp Cooler in Utah

To winterize a swamp cooler, turn off its power and water, drain the supply line and the reservoir, clean the pan, remove or dry the pads, cover the unit and close the ceiling vent. Do it before the first hard freeze, usually in October in the Salt Lake Valley, so the supply line cannot freeze, split and leak into your ceiling.

Key takeaways

  • Winterize before the first hard freeze, usually September to October in the Salt Lake Valley
  • Shut off and drain the supply line and the reservoir
  • Clean the pan and check it for rust holes
  • Cover the cooler and close the vent inside
  • A leak under the cooler needs drying fast to stop mold in the attic

When to Winterize Your Swamp Cooler in Utah

Plan to shut it down after the last warm days of fall and before nights drop below freezing. In most Salt Lake Valley years, that means late September to October. A supply line that still holds water during the first hard freeze can split and leak the next time water flows.

Step-by-Step Swamp Cooler Shutdown

  • Turn off the power at the cooler switch and the breaker.
  • Shut off the water supply at the small valve that feeds the cooler, often in the attic, under a sink or near the water heater.
  • Disconnect and drain the supply line at the cooler, and blow out any water left in it.
  • Drain the reservoir by removing the overflow or drain plug.
  • Clean the pan of scale and debris, and check for rust holes.
  • Remove or dry the pads so they do not hold water or grow mold over winter.
  • Cover the cooler with a breathable cover made for evaporative coolers.
  • Close the ceiling vent or damper inside, or add a vent cover, to stop cold drafts.

Utah's hard water builds scale fast, so cleaning the pan and float each fall adds years to the cooler.

Signs Your Cooler Is Leaking Into the Ceiling

  • A stain or bubble in the ceiling near the cooler vent
  • Drips from the vent register
  • A musty smell in the room under the cooler
  • Water running off the roof when the cooler is on
  • A leak during rain or snowmelt, even with the cooler off

If you see any of these, turn off the cooler water and call. Wet attic insulation holds water against the ceiling.

Swamp Cooler Winterizing FAQs

Can I leave water in my swamp cooler over winter?

No. Water left in the line or pan can freeze, split parts and leak into your home when it thaws.

Do I need to cover my swamp cooler?

A breathable cover keeps snow and debris out and reduces drafts. Avoid plastic tarps that trap moisture.

Should I close the vent inside?

Yes. Close the damper or add a vent cover to stop cold air and heat loss through the duct.

Can a swamp cooler cause mold in winter?

Yes, if pads stay wet or a leak soaked the attic insulation. Dry pads and fixing leaks prevent it.

When should I start my cooler again in spring?

After the last freeze, usually in May in the Salt Lake Valley. Check the line, float and pan before turning the water on.

Can I winterize my swamp cooler myself?

Most homeowners can, if they can safely reach the roof. If the roof is steep, icy or high, have an HVAC tech do it.

What happens if I forget to winterize?

The supply line or pan can freeze and crack. When it thaws, water can run through the roof opening into the attic and ceiling.

Should I change the pads when I winterize?

It is a good time to do it. Old pads full of mineral scale hold moisture and can grow mold over winter, and new pads will be ready to go in spring.

How long does a swamp cooler last?

A well-kept cooler often lasts 15 years or more. Winterizing every fall, cleaning scale and replacing a rusted pan are what keep it running and keep water out of your ceiling.

Do I need to unplug the cooler for winter?

Yes. Turn it off at the switch and the breaker or unplug it, so it cannot turn on by accident and run the pump with no water.
