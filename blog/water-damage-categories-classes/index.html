Water Damage Categories and Classes Explained

Water damage is sorted two ways under the IICRC S500 standard. The category (1, 2 or 3) tells you how contaminated the water is, which decides what can be saved. The class (1 to 4) tells you how much water soaked in and how hard it is to dry.

Key takeaways

  • Category 1 is clean water, Category 2 is gray water, Category 3 is black water
  • Category decides what can be saved; class decides how hard it is to dry
  • Clean water can become gray or black after 24 to 48 hours
  • Sewage and outside flood water are always Category 3
  • Class 4 means dense materials like hardwood and concrete

Water Damage Categories: How Dirty Is the Water?

CategoryAlso calledCommon sourcesWhat it means for cleanup
Category 1Clean waterBurst supply pipe, water heater, sink or tub overflow with clean waterMost materials can be dried and saved if work starts fast
Category 2Gray waterWashing machine, dishwasher, toilet overflow without waste, aquariumClean and treat with antimicrobial; carpet pad usually replaced
Category 3Black waterSewage, toilet overflow with waste, outside flood water, rising groundwaterRemove porous materials; clean, disinfect and dry

See sewage cleanup and flood damage restoration for Category 3 jobs.

Clean Water Doesn't Stay Clean

Water changes category as it sits. After about 24 to 48 hours, warm temperatures and contact with building materials let bacteria grow. A clean burst-pipe leak left for a few days can become Category 2 or even 3. This is why fast extraction matters so much.

Water Damage Classes: How Hard Is It to Dry?

ClassWhat is wetDrying difficulty
Class 1Part of a room; little water absorbedLowest
Class 2A whole room; carpet, pad and walls up to about 2 feetModerate
Class 3Water from above; walls, ceilings, insulation and carpet soakedHigh
Class 4Dense materials like hardwood, plaster, brick and concreteHighest; special setups and more time

The class helps set how many dehumidifiers and air movers a job needs. Learn more about structural drying.

Category and class work together. They measure different things, so any mix is possible. A clean burst pipe in an upstairs bathroom that soaked the ceiling below is Category 1, Class 3. A sewage backup on a basement slab can be Category 3, Class 4. The category sets what must be removed; the class sets the drying plan.

Why Category and Class Matter to You

  • What can be saved: carpet in Category 1 often stays; in Category 3 it goes
  • How long drying takes: a Class 4 hardwood floor takes longer than a Class 1 spill
  • Your safety: Category 3 water needs protective gear and containment
  • Your claim: adjusters use these terms; knowing them helps you follow the scope

Water Category and Class FAQs

Which category is a toilet overflow?

A toilet overflow with waste is Category 3. Clean water from the tank or a supply line is Category 1 or 2.

Can clean water turn into gray or black water?

Yes. After 24 to 48 hours, bacteria growth and contact with dirty materials can raise the category.

Is a washing machine leak Category 1 or 2?

Usually Category 2, because the water may contain soap, lint and residue.

What class is a flooded basement?

Often Class 2 or 3 depending on how high the water reached, and Class 4 for concrete and dense materials that soaked up water.

Who decides the category and class?

The restoration technician, based on the source, how long it sat, and moisture readings. It is written into the job documentation.

Is a dishwasher leak Category 1 or 2?

Usually Category 2, because dishwasher water can carry food waste and detergent.

Can Category 3 carpet ever be cleaned and kept?

No. Carpet and pad soaked by sewage or outside flood water are removed for safety.

Is rainwater or snowmelt clean water?

Not once it reaches your home. Water that runs over soil, roofs and lawns picks up dirt, fertilizer and bacteria, so outside water that enters a home is treated as Category 3.

Can hard surfaces be saved after Category 3 water?

Usually yes. Concrete, tile, metal and sealed hard surfaces can be cleaned, disinfected and dried. Porous materials like carpet, pad, drywall and insulation are removed.
