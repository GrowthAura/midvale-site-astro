How Long Does Water Damage Take to Dry?

With professional equipment, most water-damaged rooms dry in about 3 to 5 days. Hardwood floors, concrete and plaster can take 1 to 2 weeks. Without professional drying, water trapped in walls and under floors can stay wet for weeks, which is long enough for mold to grow.

Key takeaways

  • Most rooms dry in about 3 to 5 days with professional equipment
  • Hardwood, concrete and plaster can take 1 to 3 weeks
  • Mold can start in 24 to 48 hours on wet materials
  • Cold Utah basements in winter dry slower
  • Dry is proven with moisture readings, not by touch

These are typical ranges, not promises. Every job is confirmed with moisture readings.

What Slows Drying Down

  • Starting late: water spreads and soaks deeper every hour
  • Cold rooms: cold basements in a Utah winter slow evaporation
  • Dense materials: hardwood, concrete and plaster hold water longer
  • Layers: vinyl flooring, cabinets and tile trap water underneath
  • Turning equipment off: every hour off adds time
  • Not removing the source or the soaked pad and insulation

Why Utah's Dry Air Helps, but Not Enough

The Salt Lake Valley has dry air most of the year, which helps surfaces dry. But water inside walls, under floors and in concrete is sealed away from that air. Without dehumidifiers, air movers and targeted drying, those hidden areas stay wet. See how structural drying works.

The 24 to 48 Hour Mold Window

Mold can start growing on wet drywall, wood and carpet within 24 to 48 hours. You may not see it for a week or two, but it is already starting behind baseboards and under flooring. The best way to prevent mold is to remove the water and start professional drying within the first day.

What the 24 to 48 hour rule really means: it is a drying deadline, not a stopwatch for mold. EPA and CDC guidance says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, and if that did not happen, it is safest to assume mold may have started, even if you cannot see it yet.

How Pros Know It Is Really Dry

  • Take a dry reference reading from an unaffected area.
  • Map every wet area with moisture meters and a thermal camera.
  • Record readings every day.
  • Stop only when wet materials match the dry reference.

"Feels dry" is not dry. Drywall can feel dry on the surface while the back side is still wet.

Drying Time FAQs

Can water damage dry on its own?

Surface water may evaporate, but water inside walls, under floors and in pad usually does not dry on its own fast enough to stop mold and warping.

How long should fans run after water damage?

Until moisture readings show materials are dry, usually 3 to 5 days. Turning them off early leaves hidden moisture.

How fast does mold grow after water damage?

It can start in 24 to 48 hours on wet materials, and visible growth often appears within one to two weeks.

Does a dehumidifier from the store work?

It helps with room humidity but usually cannot remove enough water to dry walls, floors and concrete after real water damage.

Can I move back in while drying equipment runs?

Often yes. Drying equipment is loud, but most families stay home unless the water was sewage or flood water.

Why does it take longer to dry in winter?

Cold materials release water more slowly, and cold basements can make standard dehumidifiers ice up. Pros add heat and switch equipment to keep drying on track.

Can I speed up drying by opening windows?

In dry Utah weather it can help a little, but it can also pull in cold air that slows evaporation. Leave window decisions to the drying plan.

It has been more than 48 hours. Do I have mold?

Not for certain, but treat it as possible. We check the wettest areas, like the back of drywall, under pad and inside cabinet bases, and clean with antimicrobial before drying so mold does not get sealed in.

Why does drying take longer in a basement?

Basement concrete stays cold and holds a lot of water, and cold air carries less moisture away. That is why basements often need heat and desiccant dehumidifiers to dry on schedule.
