How to Keep Pipes From Freezing This Winter in Utah

To keep pipes from freezing, keep the heat at 55°F or higher, insulate pipes in cold spaces, disconnect garden hoses, blow out sprinkler lines, and let faucets on outside walls drip on the coldest nights. These steps take an afternoon and can prevent a burst pipe that soaks walls, floors and ceilings.

Quick answer: Pipes are at risk when outdoor temperatures drop into the low 20s °F or below, especially pipes on outside walls, in crawl spaces, garages and attics. In the Salt Lake Valley, the riskiest time is often after the cold snap, when frozen pipes thaw and the split finally leaks.

Key takeaways

  • Keep the heat at 55°F or higher, day and night
  • Disconnect garden hoses and blow out sprinkler lines before the first freeze
  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and attics
  • Drip faucets on outside walls on the coldest nights
  • Thaw frozen pipes with gentle heat, never an open flame

Where Pipes Freeze in Utah Homes

  • Exterior walls, especially under kitchen and bathroom sinks
  • Crawl spaces and unheated basements
  • Garages, including water lines to laundry or a utility sink
  • Attics, including swamp cooler supply lines
  • Hose bibs (outdoor faucets) with a hose still attached
  • Sprinkler lines and backflow devices that were not blown out
  • Service lines that are too shallow; the frost line in Salt Lake County is about 36 inches deep

10 Steps to Take Before the Cold Snap

  • Disconnect garden hoses from every outdoor faucet. A hose traps water in the faucet, even on frost-free models.
  • Shut off and drain outdoor faucets that have an inside shut-off valve.
  • Blow out sprinkler lines and insulate the backflow preventer.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in crawl spaces, garages and attics with foam pipe sleeves.
  • Use heat cable made for pipes on lines that freeze often, following the maker's instructions.
  • Seal drafts where pipes enter the house, around rim joists and crawl space vents.
  • Keep the garage door closed if water lines run through the garage.
  • Keep the heat at 55°F or higher, day and night, even when you are away.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls on very cold nights.
  • Let a slow drip run from faucets on outside walls when temperatures drop well below freezing.

Also winterize your swamp cooler before the first hard freeze.

Crawl space tip: if your home has foundation vents, close them for winter with foam blocks or vent covers so cold air does not blow across the pipes. Open them again in spring so the crawl space can breathe.

Traveling Over the Holidays?

  • Leave the heat at 55°F or higher; never turn it off
  • Ask a neighbor to check the house every day or two in a cold snap
  • Know where your main shut-off is, and consider shutting it off and draining the lines if you will be gone a long time
  • Add a water leak sensor or smart shut-off valve that alerts your phone

Signs a Pipe Is Already Frozen

  • Little or no water from a faucet when the weather is very cold
  • Frost on an exposed pipe
  • A bulge in a pipe
  • Strange smells from a drain

How to Thaw a Frozen Pipe Safely

  • Open the faucet the pipe feeds, so water can flow as the ice melts.
  • Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, heating pad or a space heater kept at a safe distance. Start near the faucet and work back.
  • Never use an open flame like a torch or propane heater.
  • Watch for leaks as it thaws. If water sprays or drips, close the main valve right away.
  • Call a plumber if you cannot find or reach the frozen section.

Frozen Pipe FAQs

What temperature do pipes freeze?

Water freezes at 32°F, but pipes in unheated spaces usually freeze when outdoor temperatures stay in the low 20s °F or below, especially overnight.

Should I drip my faucets in winter?

On the coldest nights, yes, for faucets on outside walls. Moving water is harder to freeze and a dripping faucet relieves pressure in the line.

Do frost-free outdoor faucets still freeze?

They can if a garden hose is left attached, because the hose traps water in the valve. Always disconnect hoses in fall.

Why do pipes burst after the cold snap ends?

The pipe splits while it is frozen, but the ice blocks the water. When it thaws, the water flows out through the split.

Does insurance cover frozen pipes?

Usually, if the home was heated and the burst was sudden. Claims can be denied if the home was left without heat.

Should I shut off my water when I travel in winter?

For long trips, shutting off the main valve and draining the lines lowers the risk. Keep the heat on either way, and ask someone to check the house during cold snaps.

Is heat tape safe on water pipes?

Use heat cable made for water pipes and follow the maker's instructions. Never wrap it over itself or cover it with materials it is not rated for.

What temperature should I keep my house to stop frozen pipes?

At least 55°F at all times. During a deep cold snap, many plumbers suggest 60 to 65°F, especially if pipes run through outside walls or a cold basement.

Can I flush the toilet if my pipes are frozen?

Usually yes, if the drain is not frozen and the toilet still has water in the tank. If water will not refill or drains are backing up, stop using water and call a plumber.

Should I shut off my water if a pipe is frozen?

If you see a bulge, crack or frost on a pipe, closing the main valve before it thaws is the safest choice. Then thaw it slowly and watch for leaks before turning the water back on.
