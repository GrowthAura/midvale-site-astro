Why Basements Leak When the Snow Melts in Utah

Basements leak in spring because melting snow soaks the soil around your foundation and can raise the groundwater level. That water pushes through foundation cracks, the joint where the floor meets the wall, floor drains and window wells. In the Salt Lake Valley, the risk is highest from March to May, and highest of all after big snow years.

Key takeaways

  • Snowmelt saturates soil and can raise shallow groundwater under homes
  • Window wells, downspouts and grading are the most common entry points
  • Test your sump pump and clear window wells before the melt starts
  • Homes near rivers and creeks see more groundwater seepage
  • Water from outside is treated as contaminated during cleanup

How Snowmelt Reaches Your Basement

  • Snow melts on the roof, yard and nearby slopes.
  • Water soaks into the soil around the foundation, especially where the ground slopes toward the house.
  • The soil fills up, and in some areas shallow groundwater rises closer to the surface.
  • Water pushes against the foundation (hydrostatic pressure).
  • It finds a path in: cracks, the floor-wall joint, window wells, floor drains and pipe openings.

Utah geologists note that during heavy snowmelt or persistent rain, water moves down through the soil and raises the groundwater table, which can cause problems for foundations and basements.

Big snow years are the warning sign. In the record snowpack spring of 2023, a warm spell sent runoff across the Wasatch Front, and debris-blocked storm drains flooded streets and basements in several Salt Lake, Davis and Weber County neighborhoods. Clearing the storm drain on your street helps protect every basement on the block.

Spring Prep List: Do This Before the Melt

  • Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit. Add a battery backup.
  • Clear window wells of snow, leaves and debris, and check that the drain works.
  • Add window well covers to keep snow and rain out.
  • Clean gutters and extend downspouts at least 4 to 6 feet from the house.
  • Shovel snow away from the foundation and window wells after big storms.
  • Check grading so soil slopes away from the house.
  • Seal visible foundation cracks and gaps around pipes.
  • Move valuables off basement floors and into plastic bins.
  • Add water leak sensors near the sump pit, floor drain and water heater.

If Water Gets In

  • Stay out of water if outlets or the furnace are near it.
  • Find where it is coming in, and take photos.
  • Move belongings and lift rugs.
  • Call for water removal and drying. Snowmelt water is outside water, so it is cleaned and sanitized, not just dried. See flooded basement cleanup.

Snowmelt Basement Flooding FAQs

Why does water come up through my basement floor?

When groundwater rises, it pushes up under the slab and through the joint where the floor meets the wall or through floor drains.

Does homeowners insurance cover snowmelt flooding?

Often not. Groundwater and surface water are usually excluded. Burst pipes are usually covered.

Do I need a sump pump in Utah?

Not every home needs one. Homes near rivers and creeks, or with a history of seepage, benefit most.

When is snowmelt flooding most likely in the Salt Lake Valley?

Usually March to May, especially after heavy snow years and during warm spells or rain on melting snow.

Can window well covers stop basement flooding?

They stop most snow and rain from filling the well. They do not stop groundwater rising from below.

When should I test my sump pump?

In late winter, before the melt starts. Pour water into the pit and make sure the pump starts, empties it and shuts off. Check that the float is not stuck, the discharge line did not freeze and crack, and the backup battery still holds a charge.

Can a blocked storm drain flood my basement?

Yes. When the street drain is covered with ice, leaves or trash, runoff backs up into yards, driveways and window wells. Keep the drain in front of your home clear during the melt.

How far should downspouts drain from the house?

At least 4 to 6 feet, and farther on a lot that slopes toward the house. A downspout that empties next to a window well is one of the most common causes of spring basement leaks.
