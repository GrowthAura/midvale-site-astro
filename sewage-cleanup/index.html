Sewage is Category 3 black water. It carries bacteria and viruses that make people sick. Our Midvale crew wears full protective gear, removes the sewage and everything it soaked, sanitizes every surface, and dries the area so it is safe to use again. Call (385) 488-1110.

Quick answer: Do not clean sewage yourself. Keep everyone out of the area, stop using water in the house, and call for professional cleanup. Carpet, pad and drywall touched by sewage must be removed; hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected.

Sewage in the House? Stay Out and Do This

  • Keep people and pets out of the affected area.
  • Stop using water: no flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher. Every drain adds to the backup.
  • Turn off the HVAC if the furnace or return vents are near the sewage.
  • Do not touch sewage or soaked items with bare hands.
  • Take photos from a safe distance and call (385) 488-1110.

Why Sewage Is Handled Differently in Utah

The IICRC S500 standard classifies sewage as Category 3 water, the most contaminated level. Category 3 rules mean porous materials are removed, not dried and kept. Disposal of contaminated material follows Utah rules.

Our Sewage Cleanup Steps in Midvale

  • Contain the area with plastic barriers to keep contamination from spreading.
  • Protect the crew with gloves, boots, suits and respirators.
  • Extract the sewage and solids.
  • Remove porous materials: carpet, pad, the lower part of drywall, insulation and soaked items.
  • Clean and disinfect floors, framing and surfaces with antimicrobial products.
  • Clean the air with HEPA air scrubbers.
  • Dry the structure and check readings until dry.
  • Rebuild what was removed.

Why Sewer Backups Happen in Midvale Homes

  • Tree roots growing into older clay sewer laterals, common in mid-century homes
  • Grease and wipes clogging the line
  • Heavy storms overloading city sewer mains
  • Collapsed or sagging pipe between your home and the street
  • Basement floor drains as the lowest exit point, so backups show up there first

A plumber can camera-inspect your line and add a backwater valve to block future backups.

Is it your line or the city's? In most Salt Lake County cities, the homeowner is responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to about the property line, and the city or sewer district owns the main in the street. If several neighbors back up at once, the blockage is likely in the main: call your sewer provider right away, and keep photos for any claim.

Sewage Backup Cleanup FAQs

Is sewage backup dangerous to breathe?

Sewage gives off gases and can spread bacteria in the air. Keep the area closed off and ventilate only once cleanup starts.

Can carpet be saved after a sewage backup?

No. Carpet and pad soaked with sewage must be removed and disposed of.

Is a toilet overflow sewage?

A toilet overflow with waste is Category 3. Clean water from a running tank is usually Category 1 or 2. See water categories explained.

Does insurance cover sewer backup?

Often only with a sewer backup rider added to the homeowners policy. Check yours.

How long does sewage cleanup take?

Cleanup and disinfecting often take 1 to 2 days. Drying adds 3 to 5 days, depending on the size of the area.

What is a backwater valve?

A one-way valve on your sewer line that lets waste flow out but closes when sewage tries to flow back in. A plumber can add one to homes that have backed up before.

Can I stay in the house after a sewage backup?

You can usually stay in clean areas if the affected room is sealed off and you avoid using water. Children, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system should stay away until cleanup is done.

What are the warning signs of a coming sewer backup?

Slow drains in several fixtures, gurgling toilets, water rising in the basement floor drain and sewer smells. Stop using water and call a plumber before it overflows.
