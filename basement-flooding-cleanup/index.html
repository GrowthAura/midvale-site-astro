Flooded Basement Cleanup in Midvale

When your basement floods, our Midvale crew pumps out the water, removes wet carpet pad and drywall that cannot be saved, dries the floor and walls, and shows you where the water came in. Call (385) 488-1110, day or night.

Quick answer: Basement water usually comes from one of five places: a burst pipe, a failed water heater or sump pump, a window well, a sewer backup, or groundwater after snowmelt. The source decides how the cleanup is done, because sewage and outside water are treated as contaminated.

Midvale Basement Flooding? Safety First

  • Do not walk into water if outlets, cords or the furnace are under water. Call the power company if you cannot reach the breaker safely.
  • Stop the source if it is plumbing: close the main shut-off valve.
  • Keep out of sewage. Gray or brown water with an odor means a sewer backup.
  • Take photos, then call (385) 488-1110.

Utah homeowners see the most basement water between October and March, when rain and melting snow saturate the ground.

How groundwater gets in: when the soil around your foundation is full of water, it pushes against the walls and floor. This is called hydrostatic pressure. Water then comes through foundation cracks, the joint where the floor meets the wall, and up through floor drains.

Clay soil makes it worse. Many Salt Lake Valley lots have heavy clay that holds water against the foundation for days after a storm or snowmelt, so seepage can keep coming after the rain stops.

Our Basement Water Removal Process in Midvale

  • Pump out standing water with submersible pumps and truck-mounted extraction.
  • Remove soaked carpet pad and any drywall above the wet line that cannot be dried (a flood cut).
  • Clean and treat concrete, framing and walls with antimicrobial.
  • Dry with dehumidifiers and air movers; concrete and cold basements may need desiccant and heat.
  • Verify dry with daily moisture readings.
  • Rebuild walls, flooring and trim.

How to Keep Your Basement From Flooding Again

  • Test your sump pump each fall and add a battery backup
  • Clear window wells and add covers
  • Make sure soil slopes away from the foundation
  • Extend downspouts at least 4 to 6 feet from the house
  • Ask a plumber about a backwater valve if sewage has backed up before

Flooded Basement FAQs

Why does my basement flood when snow melts?

Melting snow saturates the soil. Water then pushes through foundation cracks, the joint where the floor meets the wall, and window wells.

Can a finished basement be saved?

Often, yes, if the water was clean and we start within a day. Carpet pad and the lower part of drywall are the parts most often replaced.

What if my sump pump failed?

Water from a sump pit is treated as gray water. We extract it, clean the floor and walls, and dry everything. Replace or repair the pump before the next storm.

How long before mold grows in a basement?

Mold can start in 24 to 48 hours. Basements are cool and damp, so fast drying matters most here.

Is basement flooding covered by insurance?

Burst pipes and water heater failures are usually covered. Groundwater and outside flooding often are not, and sewer backup may need a separate rider.

Why did my basement flood when the power went out?

A sump pump without a battery backup stops during an outage, which often happens in the same storm that fills the pit. A battery backup keeps it running.

Can a sump pump discharge line freeze?

Yes. In Utah, a discharge line that is not buried below the frost line (about 36 inches) or sloped to drain can freeze, so the pump runs but the water has nowhere to go. Check where the line exits before winter.

Does flood insurance cover water coming up through the basement floor?

Often not. Groundwater seeping up through the slab is usually excluded by both homeowners and flood policies. Read your policy and ask your agent before you need it.
