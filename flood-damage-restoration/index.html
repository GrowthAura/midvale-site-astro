When storm water, a flash flood or spring runoff gets into your home, our Midvale crew removes the water and mud, sanitizes everything it touched, dries the structure and rebuilds what had to come out. Call (385) 488-1110, day or night.

Quick answer: Water that comes in from outside is treated as Category 3, the most contaminated level, because it carries soil, bacteria and chemicals. Carpet, pad and soaked drywall are removed, and every surface it touched is cleaned and disinfected before drying.

Flood Water in Your Midvale Home? Do This First

  • Stay out of the water if power is on in the area or the water came from a storm drain or creek.
  • Turn off power at the breaker only from a dry spot. Otherwise call the power company.
  • Do not use fans or the HVAC to dry it yourself; they can spread contamination.
  • Take photos and video before moving anything.

Why monsoon floods catch people off guard: a late-summer cloudburst can drop more rain in an hour than storm drains, gutters and window wells can carry. Water then runs to the lowest point, usually a basement door, window well or garage, within minutes.

Why Outside Flood Water Is Treated as Category 3

The IICRC S500 standard classes outside flood water as Category 3, the same level as sewage. It can carry soil bacteria, fertilizer, oil and sewage from overloaded lines. That is why flood cleanup is about sanitizing, not just drying. See water categories explained.

Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Midvale

  • Safety check: power, gas and structure.
  • Pump out water and mud with submersible pumps and truck-mounted extraction.
  • Remove what cannot be saved: carpet, pad, insulation and drywall above the water line (a flood cut).
  • Clean and disinfect floors, framing and walls with antimicrobial.
  • Dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers, checked daily.
  • Rebuild drywall, flooring and trim.

What Affects How Long Flood Cleanup Takes

  • How deep the water got and how many rooms it reached
  • How long it sat before extraction started
  • What it soaked: carpet and drywall come out; concrete and framing can be cleaned and dried
  • Finished or unfinished basement: finished spaces need more removal and rebuild
  • Silt and mud: heavy debris adds cleaning time before drying can start

Flood Insurance vs Homeowners Insurance

Most homeowners policies do not cover outside flood water. That usually needs separate flood insurance. Water from inside the home, like a burst pipe, is usually covered by the homeowners policy. We document everything so you can file with the right policy.

Flood Damage FAQs

Is flood water always contaminated?

Yes. Water that comes from outside or rises from the ground is treated as Category 3 because it can carry bacteria and chemicals.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?

Usually not for outside flood water. That needs flood insurance. Inside sources like burst pipes are usually covered.

What can be saved after a flood?

Hard, non-porous items and most wood framing can be cleaned and dried. Carpet, pad, drywall below the water line and soaked upholstery are usually removed.

How do you sanitize after a flood?

We remove soaked materials, clean surfaces, apply antimicrobial, and dry the structure while HEPA air scrubbers clean the air.

How long does flood cleanup take?

Pump-out and removal often take 1 to 2 days. Drying usually adds 3 to 5 days, then rebuild.

Should I rip out wet drywall myself after a flood?

It is safer to wait. Flood water is contaminated, and cutting drywall spreads it. Wear gloves and boots if you must move items, and leave removal to a crew with protective gear.

Is it safe to stay in my house after a flood?

Stay out of flooded areas until power is checked and the water is removed. If the flood reached living areas or the furnace, it is often safer to stay elsewhere until cleanup is done.

Should I get a mold check after a flood?

Yes, if water sat for more than a day or reached inside walls. Flood water carries mold spores and nutrients, so we check the wall cavities and framing before anything is closed up.

What should I throw away after a flood?

Food, medicine, cosmetics and baby items that touched flood water, plus mattresses, pillows, stuffed toys and soaked paper. Photograph everything before it goes out so your insurer can see it.

How do I protect my home before monsoon season?

Clear gutters and window well drains, extend downspouts, make sure soil slopes away from the house, test the sump pump and store valuables off the basement floor.
