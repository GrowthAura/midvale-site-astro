Water Damage Insurance Claim Help in Midvale

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or a failed water heater. Our Midvale crew documents the damage with photos, moisture maps and daily drying logs, and shares them with your adjuster so your claim has the proof it needs. Call (385) 488-1110.

Your First Steps for a Strong Claim

  • Stop the water and make the area safe.
  • Take photos and video of every wet room, the source and damaged items before cleanup.
  • Call your insurance company to open a claim and write down the claim number.
  • Start mitigation right away. Drying now prevents mold and further damage.
  • Keep damaged items until the adjuster says you can discard them, or photograph and list them.
  • Save receipts for hotel stays, meals or emergency purchases if you had to leave home.

How We Document Your Water Damage Claim

  • Photos of every affected area, before, during and after
  • The cause and water category, in writing
  • A moisture map from day one and daily drying logs
  • A list of removed materials and why they were removed
  • A line-by-line repair scope your adjuster can match to their estimate

This is the proof adjusters look for when deciding what was damaged and why.

Working With Your Adjuster

We can meet your adjuster on site, walk through the moisture map, and answer questions about what was removed and why. If the adjuster's estimate leaves out work the readings show is needed, we provide the documentation so you can ask for a review of the scope.

Common Water Damage Claim Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting to start drying until the adjuster visits
  • Throwing out damaged items before photographing and listing them
  • Painting over stains before the area is confirmed dry
  • Not asking for the reason in writing if part of the claim is denied
  • Missing the paperwork: no photos, no readings, no list of removed materials

Water Damage Insurance FAQs

Should I call insurance or a restoration company first?

Stop the water and call for mitigation right away, then open your claim the same day. Waiting for the insurance company before drying can make the damage, and the claim, worse.

Do I have to use the restoration company my insurer recommends?

No. You can choose your own restoration company. Your insurer may suggest one, but the choice is yours.

What is a sewer backup rider?

It is an add-on to a homeowners policy that covers damage from water backing up through drains or the sewer line. Many standard policies leave it out.

Will my claim be denied for a slow leak?

Policies often exclude damage from leaks that went on for a long time. Sudden events are more likely to be covered.

What if my claim is denied?

Ask the insurer for the reason in writing and review your policy with your agent. You can also contact the Utah Insurance Department with questions about how a claim was handled.

How soon should I report water damage to my insurer?

As soon as you can, ideally the same day. Most policies require prompt notice, and your policy lists the exact rules.

Should I get more than one repair scope?

You can. Your adjuster writes an estimate, and we provide a detailed scope based on moisture readings and photos. Comparing them helps make sure nothing is missed.

Should I be home when the adjuster visits?

Yes, if you can. Being there lets you point out damage the adjuster might miss and ask questions on the spot. We can be there too, with the moisture map and photos.

Does my policy pay for a hotel if I have to leave?

Many policies include additional living expenses, also called loss of use, when a covered loss makes the home unlivable. Keep every receipt and ask your adjuster what your policy allows.

What is a public adjuster?

A public adjuster works for you, not the insurance company, and is usually paid a share of the settlement. If you hire one, the Utah Insurance Department suggests checking that they are registered with the department and asking for references first.

Can I add items to my claim later?

Yes. If you remember damaged items after the first list, contact your insurer and add them. That is one reason we photograph and list everything we remove.
