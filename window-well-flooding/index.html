Window Well Flooding Cleanup in Midvale

When a basement window well fills with snow or rain, the water rises above the window sill and pours into your basement through the frame. Our Midvale crew removes the water, dries the wall, carpet and framing below the window, and helps you stop it from happening again. Call (385) 488-1110.

Quick answer: Window wells flood when water collects faster than it can drain: a clogged or missing drain, snow that melts during the day, soil that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying next to the well. Outside water is treated as contaminated, so carpet pad under the window is usually replaced.

Water Coming Through a Basement Window? Do This Now

  • Bail or pump water out of the well if you can do it safely from outside.
  • Clear snow and leaves from the well and its drain.
  • Move belongings away from the wet wall and lift rugs.
  • Do not open the window to drain it inward.
  • Take photos, inside and out, then call (385) 488-1110.

Many Midvale homes built from the 1970s to the 1990s have finished basements with egress window wells, so one blocked drain can soak a bedroom or family room.

It can happen in minutes. Local news has covered Salt Lake County basements where a full window well pushed water under the window or broke the glass during a single storm, including a Cottonwood Heights home that took on nearly four feet of water. Deep daylight wells hold the most water, so they need the best drainage.

Our Window Well Water Cleanup Process in Midvale

  • Extract water from carpet, pad and the floor.
  • Remove wet carpet pad and drywall below the window that cannot be dried.
  • Clean and apply antimicrobial to the wall, framing and floor.
  • Dry the wall cavity and slab with dehumidifiers and air movers.
  • Check readings daily until everything is dry.
  • Replace drywall, baseboards and carpet pad.

How Midvale Homeowners Can Stop Window Well Flooding

  • Install clear window well covers to keep snow and rain out while letting light in
  • Clean the well and drain every fall and after big storms
  • Pour a bucket of water into the well to test the drain; it should empty within minutes
  • Fix the grading so soil slopes away from the house
  • Extend downspouts away from window wells
  • Reseal the window frame if caulk is cracked

Window Well Flooding FAQs

Why does my window well fill with water?

Usually a clogged or missing drain, snow that melts and refreezes, or grading and downspouts sending water toward the well.

Should window wells have drains?

A drain or deep gravel base helps water soak away. If your well has none, ask a contractor about adding one.

Do window well covers stop flooding?

Covers keep snow and most rain out, which prevents most window well floods. They do not stop groundwater rising from below.

Can the carpet be saved after window well flooding?

The carpet itself can sometimes be cleaned and dried. The pad under it is usually replaced because outside water is contaminated.

Is window well flooding covered by insurance?

It depends on the policy. Surface water entering the home is often excluded. We document everything so you can file if it is covered.

Can water in a window well break the glass?

Yes. A well full of water presses hard on the window, and many older basement windows are not watertight. Water usually leaks under the frame first, but the glass can break if the well fills quickly.

What if the water came from my neighbor's yard?

Take photos of where the water came from, and tell your insurer and your neighbor. Who pays depends on your policies and the cause. Our moisture readings and photos support your claim either way.

Do I need a window well cover rated for snow?

In Midvale winters, yes. Pick a cover built for snow load, so it does not crack or cave in under a heavy storm.
