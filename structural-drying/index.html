Structural Drying in Midvale: Inside Walls, Floors and Ceilings

Structural drying removes the water that soaked into drywall, framing, subfloors and concrete after the standing water is gone. Our Midvale crew uses dehumidifiers, air movers and wall cavity drying, and we take readings every day until each material reaches its dry standard. Call (385) 488-1110.

(385) 488-1110

Quick answer: A house is not dry when it feels dry. Drywall can feel dry on the surface while the back side and the wood behind it are still wet. Professional drying measures moisture inside the materials and keeps going until the numbers match a dry reference reading.

What Is Structural Drying?

Water moves from wet to dry and from warm to cool. After extraction, water stays trapped in porous materials. Structural drying speeds up evaporation and pulls that moisture out of the air before it can soak back in.

Drying Equipment We Bring to Midvale Homes

EquipmentWhat it does

How We Prove Your Home Is Dry

  • On day one we take a dry reference reading from an unaffected wall or floor.
  • We mark and measure every wet area and record it on a moisture map.
  • We check readings, humidity and equipment every day and adjust the setup.
  • We stop only when wet materials match the dry reference.

You get the daily logs and photos for your insurance claim.

Why We Track Grains per Pound, Not Just Humidity

Grains per pound (GPP) is the actual weight of water in the air. Unlike relative humidity, it does not change with temperature. A GPP number that falls each day shows the dehumidifiers are winning.

We also watch cold rooms closely. Standard refrigerant dehumidifiers can ice up in cold Utah basements in winter, so we switch to LGR or desiccant units and add heat. And we never over-dry hardwood, which can crack it.

Water Damage Drying Classes 1 to 4 Explained

The IICRC S500 standard groups drying jobs by how much water is in the materials.

ClassWhat is wetHow hard to dry
Class 1A small area, little water absorbedFastest
Class 2A whole room, carpet and lower wallsModerate
Class 3Water came from above: walls, ceilings, insulationSlower
Class 4Dense materials: hardwood, plaster, brick, concreteSlowest, special setups

How Long Drying Takes in Midvale and Salt Lake County Homes

Most rooms dry in 3 to 5 days. Utah's dry air helps, but cold basements in winter slow evaporation, so we add heat and desiccant drying when needed. Hardwood floors and concrete can take longer. Read more: how long does water damage take to dry.

Drying is planned, not guessed. On day one we work out how many dehumidifiers and air movers the room needs from its size, the drying class and the readings. As materials dry, we move or remove equipment so the last wet spots get the most airflow.

Structural Drying FAQs

Do the walls have to be cut open?

Not always. Wall cavity drying lets us dry many walls through small holes. We remove drywall when it is contaminated, sagging or will not dry.

Why do the fans run for days?

Water inside wood and drywall comes out slowly. Turning equipment off early leaves moisture behind, which leads to mold and warping.

Can I turn the equipment off at night?

Please leave it running. Every hour it is off adds time to the job.

Is the drying equipment loud?

Air movers make a steady hum. We place them to keep walkways clear and noise down in bedrooms.

Will drying stop mold?

Fast, measured drying is the best way to prevent mold. If mold has already started, we handle it with mold remediation.

Should I open the windows to help it dry?

Sometimes. On a dry Salt Lake Valley summer day, outside air can help, and we check it with a hygrometer first. In winter, during an inversion or on a rainy day, open windows let in cold or damp air and slow the job, so keep them closed unless we say otherwise.

How do you decide how much equipment to use?

We size it from the square footage, how wet the materials are (the drying class) and the air readings. Too little equipment drags the job out; too much wastes power and can over-dry wood.

What happens if drying stops too early?

Water left inside framing, subfloor or the back of drywall can grow mold, warp floors and cause odors weeks later. That is why we finish only when readings match the dry reference.
